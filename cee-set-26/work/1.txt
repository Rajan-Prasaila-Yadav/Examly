3: Zoology  |  Questions 1–40
4: (c) Sphincter of Oddi
5: The sphincter of Oddi, also known as the hepatopancreatic sphincter, is a muscular valve that regulates the flow of bile and pancreatic juice into the duodenum. The sphincter of Boyden surrounds the terminal part of the common bile duct.
6: Review note: The book describes Boyden’s sphincter as surrounding the opening of the common bile duct into the pancreatic duct. The explanation here uses the terminal common bile duct wording; the selected answer remains (c).
7: (d) Paneth cells
8: Paneth cells secrete lysozyme and are found in the intestinal crypts. The book additionally mentions S cells, CCK cells and K cells, and identifies Kupffer cells as macrophages of the liver.
9: Review note: The source groups S, CCK and K cells under argentaffin cells. These are distinct enteroendocrine cell types; this ancillary classification is not needed to identify Paneth cells as the answer.
10: (c) Between the lens and the retina
11: The vitreous chamber lies behind the lens and contains vitreous humour, which helps maintain the shape of the eyeball.
12: (c) Tectorial membrane
13: The tectorial membrane is a gelatinous structure within the cochlea of the inner ear and plays a crucial role in hearing.
14: Review note: The scan calls the tectorial membrane ‘cellular’. The standard description is acellular; the selected structure remains the tectorial membrane.
15: (b) Pons
16: The apneustic and pneumotaxic centres are present in the pons. They respectively stimulate and inhibit inspiratory activity. The inspiratory centre is in the dorsal medulla; the expiratory centre is in the ventral medulla.
17: (b) Nodes of Ranvier
18: Nodes of Ranvier are small gaps in the myelin sheath. They permit ion exchange and speed the propagation of nerve impulses.
19: Review note: The scan’s phrase ‘energy deficiency’ appears to be a typographical error. It has not been repeated as a function of the nodes of Ranvier.
20: (a) Broca’s area
21: Broca’s area is located in the frontal lobe of the dominant hemisphere, usually the left. It is responsible for speech production and language processing, controlling motor functions involved in the muscle movements needed for speaking.

23: (c) SA node
24: The SA node lies near the opening of the superior vena cava in the right atrium. It initiates the heartbeat and acts as the natural pacemaker.
25: Review note: The source’s extra statements about the AV node and non-rhythmic conduction in the bundle of His are not required for this question. The keyed answer is the SA node, the normal pacemaker.
26: (b) Infundibular stalk
27: The pituitary gland is attached to the hypothalamus by the infundibular stalk and lies in the sella turcica of the sphenoid bone.
28: Blood vessels transport hormones but do not form this attachment. The malleus is an auditory ossicle of the middle ear. Pituicytes are supporting cells of the posterior pituitary, not the connecting structure.
29: (b) Corpus luteum
30: An LH surge triggers ovulation, causing the mature Graafian follicle to rupture and release the ovum. The ruptured follicle becomes the corpus luteum, a temporary endocrine gland or “yellow body”.
31: Progesterone prepares and maintains the endometrium for implantation. Relaxin helps relax uterine muscles and pelvic ligaments during pregnancy. Inhibin inhibits FSH secretion from the anterior pituitary.
32: (c) Between fertilization and parturition
33: Gestation is the interval from fertilization to parturition. During this period the embryo develops into a fetus and completes its growth in the uterus. The book quotes approximately 280 days, 40 weeks or 9 months for human pregnancy; see the clarification below.
34: Review note: The quoted 280 days or 40 weeks is conventionally measured from the last menstrual period. The interval from fertilization is about 266 days or 38 weeks. The definition asked for still corresponds to option (c).

36: (c) Carbon dioxide concentration
37: An increase in the carbon dioxide concentration of blood stimulates the respiratory centre in the medulla oblongata and produces the urge to breathe.
38: (b) Nephrons
39: Nephrons are the structural and functional units of the kidney. They filter blood, reabsorb useful substances and form urine.
40: (b) Hypertonic
41: Hypertonic urine has a higher solute concentration than blood plasma, the typical condition described in the book. Isotonic or equitonic solutions have the same osmotic concentration as the comparison solution. Hypotonic urine is more dilute and may occur after drinking excess water.
42: (c) Tuna
43: The book identifies tuna as the fish associated with extensive migration across ocean basins, and mentions the Earth’s magnetic field, ocean currents and temperature gradients as navigation cues.
44: (b) Biodiversity hotspot
45: A biodiversity hotspot combines high endemism with substantial habitat loss. A polar biome is defined by its cold climate. A Ramsar site is a wetland of international importance. A biosphere reserve supports conservation and sustainable use; it is not synonymous with a hotspot.
46: (c) Beer
47: Beer is a fermented beverage produced by yeast fermentation. Cheese, yogurt, bread and buns are fermented foods; milk itself is not a fermented product.

50: Plasmodium malariae causes quartan malaria, characterized by a fever cycle of approximately 72 hours. The other listed species are associated with tertian malaria.

52: (a) Only one earthworm develops
53: The book states that metamorphosis is absent in the earthworm. A cocoon may contain several fertilized eggs, but only one embryo develops in the account used here.
54: (b) 6th segment behind
55: Lymph glands occur on both sides of the dorsal blood vessel from the 26th segment backwards, generally one pair per segment. They are small, whitish structures thought to produce phagocytic cells or corpuscles.

57: (a) To protect the eyes underwater
58: The nictitating membrane is a transparent third eyelid. It protects a frog’s eyes while underwater or swimming, and from dust and injury on land, while still permitting vision.
59: (a) 3 digestive enzymes and 2 hormones
60: The frog pancreas is a mixed gland with both exocrine and endocrine functions. The book lists three main digestive enzymes and two hormones, insulin and glucagon.
61: (d) Echinodermata
62: Adult echinoderms, such as starfish, exhibit radial symmetry. Other examples of radially symmetrical animals include jellyfish and sea anemones.
63: (b) Miracidium and metacercaria
64: Miracidium enters the snail, the intermediate host. Cercariae eventually leave the snail and develop into metacercariae, the stage that infects the primary host.
65: (b) Spider
66: The spider belongs to class Arachnida. Housefly, cockroach and mosquito belong to class Insecta.
67: (b) They have a body plan with two germ layers and radial symmetry.
68: Coelenterates, or cnidarians, are diploblastic, radially symmetrical and acoelomate, with a gastrovascular cavity.
69: (c) Different function but same structure
70: Homologous organs have the same basic anatomical structure but perform different functions. Examples include the forelimbs of humans and bats, and the mouthparts of cockroaches and butterflies.
71: (c) Natural selection
72: Darwin proposed natural selection as the mechanism of evolution, often summarized as “survival of the fittest”.
73: Germplasm theory is associated with Weismann; use and disuse with Lamarck; mutation theory with Hugo de Vries.
74: (d) Tool maker
75: Homo habilis means “handy man” or “tool maker”, reflecting its association with simple stone tools. Homo sapiens refers to modern humans; Homo erectus means “upright man”.
76: (b) Stratified cuboidal epithelium
77: Stratified cuboidal epithelium consists of multiple cell layers with cuboidal cells in the superficial layer. Stratified epithelia are named after their superficial cells. Mammary glands contain milk-secreting alveoli lined by cuboidal cells and surrounded by myoepithelial cells; their larger ducts may have stratified cuboidal epithelium.
78: (d) Hyaline cartilage
79: Hyaline cartilage caps the ends of bones at joints and is then called articular cartilage. Its smooth surface reduces friction and permits smooth joint movement.
80: (c) Presence of actin and myosin filaments
81: Skeletal muscle fibres are cylindrical; visceral smooth-muscle cells are spindle-shaped. Both contain the contractile proteins actin, in thin filaments, and myosin, in thick filaments.
82: (d) Areolar connective tissue
83: Mast cells occur mainly in areolar connective tissue, especially around blood vessels and beneath the skin. They participate in inflammation and allergic reactions by releasing histamine and heparin.
84: (b) Pulmonary tuberculosis
85: Chronic cough, blood-streaked sputum, night sweats and weight loss are characteristic features of pulmonary tuberculosis caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis.
86: (b) AIDS-related complex (ARC)
87: The book uses AIDS-related complex (ARC) for an early symptomatic stage of HIV infection with fever, fatigue, weight loss and lymphadenopathy, before full-blown AIDS or severe opportunistic infections.
88: (b) Tumor suppressor genes
89: Tumour suppressor genes inhibit uncontrolled cell proliferation. They regulate the cell cycle, promote DNA repair and may trigger apoptosis in damaged cells.
90: (d) Cell-mediated immunity
91: The thymus supports the maturation of T lymphocytes, which are essential for cell-mediated immunity. Absence of the thymus impairs T-cell development and responses to infected or abnormal cells.
92: Botany  |  Questions 41–80
93: (c) Sex chromosome
94: Sex chromosomes determine the chromosomal sex of a human male. The usual male complement is XY.
95: (d) Antirrhinum
96: Incomplete dominance occurs in Antirrhinum, or snapdragon. Crossing red-flowered and white-flowered plants produces pink-flowered offspring.
97: (d) Co-dominance
98: In the AB blood group, both A and B alleles are expressed in the phenotype. This is codominance.
99: (c) Polygenic inheritance
100: Human height shows continuous variation because it is influenced by many genes, or polygenes. It is an example of polygenic inheritance.
101: (b) Polygenic inheritance
102: The ratio 1:4:6:4:1 is a characteristic F2 pattern for a two-locus additive model of polygenic inheritance, in which several genes contribute cumulative effects to a trait.
103: (a) XXX
104: The book’s term “super female” refers to the XXX chromosome complement, which can arise through nondisjunction of sex chromosomes.
105: (a) Disaccharides
106: Maltose, lactose and sucrose are disaccharides, each composed of two monosaccharide units.
107: (a) Simple lipids
108: (b) Endodermis
109: The endodermis is called the starch sheath because its cells often contain abundant starch grains. The pericycle participates in lateral-root formation; the epidermis is the outer protective layer; the hypodermis lies just below it and provides support.
110: (b) Vascular bundles closed
111: Monocot stem vascular bundles are closed, meaning that vascular cambium is absent. Without this cambium, ordinary secondary thickening does not occur.
112: (a) Xylem is sandwiched between phloem
113: A bicollateral vascular bundle contains two regions of phloem, one outside and one inside, with xylem between them.
114: (b) Bacterial multiplication is reduced
115: Refrigeration slows bacterial growth and reproduction rather than completely killing bacteria. The slower multiplication helps food remain fresh for longer.
116: (a) Rabies virus
117: Rabies virus has a characteristic bullet shape and belongs to the family Rhabdoviridae. The book contrasts it with spherical varicella virus, brick-shaped variola virus and icosahedral poliovirus.
118: (b) Cyphellae
119: Cyphellae are small pore-like structures on the lower surface of some lichens. They allow gaseous exchange, including movement of oxygen and carbon dioxide.
120: (c) Mushroom
121: Fairy rings arise from the radial outward growth of fungal mycelium. Mushrooms, commonly basidiomycetes, appear in a ring as the mycelium spreads.
122: (d) Dispersal of spores
123: Elaters in liverworts and pseudo-elaters in hornworts respond to humidity by twisting and untwisting. Their hygroscopic movements assist spore dispersal.
124: (c) Ferns
125: Antheridia with a jacket containing two ring cells are characteristic of ferns, or pteridophytes.
126: (a) Scales of the cones
127: In conifers, sporangia are borne on the scales of male and female cones: microsporangia in male cones and megasporangia associated with ovules in female cones.
128: (b) Unicostate parallel
129: Musa is a monocot. Its leaves show unicostate parallel venation: a single prominent midrib with parallel veins extending on either side.
130: Review note: The question paper has identical wording for options (c) and (d), ‘Divergent reticulate’. The book key (b), unicostate parallel, is unaffected.
131: (b) Taiga
132: The taiga, or boreal forest, is the largest terrestrial biome. Its broad belt of coniferous forest extends across North America, Europe and Asia.
133: (c) Community

135: (b) Industrial wastes
136: Industrial effluents may contain toxic chemicals, heavy metals and untreated wastes. Their discharge can damage aquatic life and make water unsuitable for human use.
137: (a) Microconsumers
138: Bacteria act as decomposers, breaking down dead organic matter. They are therefore described as microconsumers.
139: (b) Release ATP
140: Mitochondria produce ATP through cellular respiration. ATP acts as the cell’s energy currency, giving mitochondria the description “powerhouse of the cell”.
141: (d) Ribosomes
142: Ribosomes are attached to the surface of rough endoplasmic reticulum and give it its rough appearance.
143: (c) Division of nucleus
144: Karyokinesis is division of the nucleus. Cytokinesis is division of the cytoplasm, separating the cell into two daughter cells.
145: (d) Stem and root tip both
146: Somatic cell division is mitosis. It occurs actively in meristematic tissues, including the root tip and stem or shoot tip.
147: (b) G2-phase
148: During G2, the cell prepares for mitosis and synthesizes proteins needed for spindle-fibre formation, supporting chromosome separation.
149: (c) Two

152: (c) Stroma outside photosynthetic lamellae
153: The dark reaction, or Calvin cycle, takes place in the chloroplast stroma. It does not directly require light, but uses ATP and NADPH produced by the light reactions.
154: (b) Do not absorb but reflect green light
155: Chlorophyll absorbs red and blue light efficiently. Green light is largely reflected, making leaves appear green.
156: (a) Climacteric
157: The climacteric is the sharp rise in respiration during the ripening of fruits such as banana. It is associated with increased ethylene production and changes in softness, sweetness and colour.
158: (c) Night
159: Skototropism is growth or movement towards darkness, in contrast to growth towards light. It occurs in some climbing plants and other organisms.
160: (a) Dissolves in water and alters osmotic balance
161: Glucose is highly soluble in water. Storing a large amount as free glucose would raise the osmotic concentration of cell sap, draw excess water into cells and disturb osmotic balance.
162: (a) Circinotropous
163: In Opuntia, the funiculus grows around the ovule, causing it to turn through a complete circle. This ovule is circinotropous.
164: (c) Reproduction without fusion of gametes
165: Apomixis is asexual reproduction through seeds without fertilization or fusion of male and female gametes. The embryo may develop from an egg cell or other cells without the usual sexual process.
166: (a) Totipotency
167: Totipotency is the ability of a plant cell to develop into a whole plant. Gene cloning copies particular genes; tissue culture uses totipotency; regeneration refers more generally to regrowth of parts.
168: (c) Genetic engineering
169: Genetic engineering introduces specific genetic changes. Selection is a traditional method, mutation generates random changes, and tissue culture is primarily a propagation technique.
170: (c) Distillate of fermented molasses
171: Rum is obtained by distilling the fermented products of molasses.
172: (b) Hibiscus rosa-sinensis
173: China rose is Hibiscus rosa-sinensis, a member of Malvaceae. Gossypium hirsutum is cotton, Abelmoschus esculentus is okra, and Hibiscus cannabinus is kenaf.
